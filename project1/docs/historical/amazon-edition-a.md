# Project 1 Valuation — Edition A

## Decision and user

I am looking at Amazon to decide whether it is worth continuing with a full valuation for Project 1. The intended user is me acting as a student equity-research analyst. My decision is whether to initiate research now, wait for more information, or stop considering the company.

## Company and valuation date

- Company: Amazon.com, Inc.
- Ticker: AMZN
- Valuation date: September 1, 2026
- Initial decision: Watch/defer

## Initial thesis

My starting opinion is **watch/defer**. Amazon is growing, especially in AWS, and has a large amount of cash. However, it also has a large amount of debt and is spending heavily on technology and infrastructure. I want to understand whether its future cash flow can support that spending before making a stronger decision.

## Known evidence

Amazon's Form 10-Q covers the quarter ended June 30, 2026 and was filed July 30, 2026. The accession number is 0001018724-26-000026. The statements are in U.S. dollars and mainly reported in millions.

Amazon reported $200.606 billion of total net sales for the three months ended June 30, 2026, compared with $167.702 billion in the same quarter of 2025. AWS sales were $42.232 billion, compared with $30.873 billion in the same quarter of 2025. Amazon said AWS sales increased 37% because of increased customer usage, partly offset by pricing changes.

At June 30, 2026, Amazon reported $78.213 billion in cash and cash equivalents and $44.775 billion in marketable securities. It reported $132.995 billion as the face value of long-term debt, including a $3.330 billion current portion. Long-term debt shown on the balance sheet after the current portion and issuance adjustments was $128.894 billion.

The diluted-share figure was 10.903 billion weighted-average shares for the second quarter of 2026. I am using diluted weighted-average shares because they include the effect of potentially dilutive stock awards and are more complete than basic shares for a per-share valuation.

Primary source: Amazon.com, Inc., Form 10-Q for the quarter ended June 30, 2026, filed July 30, 2026: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1018724/000101872426000026/amzn-20260630.htm

## Consequential assumptions

- AWS will continue growing, although probably not at exactly the current 37% rate.
- Amazon will turn enough of its sales into operating cash flow to support its investments.
- Technology and infrastructure spending will eventually produce useful returns.
- I will treat cash, debt, leases, and investments consistently when moving from enterprise value to equity value.

## Unknowns

- I do not yet know what a reasonable long-term AWS growth rate should be.
- I do not yet know how much of Amazon's current investment spending is temporary.
- I need to decide how leases and non-operating investments should be handled in the valuation.
- I do not yet have a discount rate or a finished free-cash-flow forecast.
- I do not yet know whether Amazon looks overvalued or undervalued at the valuation date.

## Research plan

1. Review Amazon's cash-flow statement and separate normal spending from unusually high investment spending.
2. Compare AWS growth and margins with earlier periods.
3. Build simple revenue, operating-margin, and free-cash-flow forecasts.
4. Estimate a discount rate and terminal-growth rate.
5. Complete the enterprise-to-equity bridge using consistent cash, debt, lease, and share-count definitions.
6. Test how the result changes when AWS growth, margins, and the discount rate change.
