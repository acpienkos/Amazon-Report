# Home down-payment allocation decision

OpenAI Codex assisted with modeling, checks, and drafting. Andrew Pienkos owns the final analysis and decision upon review.

Education, not investment advice.

**Ask the committee to reject live allocation through this process (0%).** Reconsider a 0–5% global-equity pilot only after the client confirms the purchase timing and acceptable loss, fund exposure and cash availability checks pass, and an independent holdout supports the 4% net objective under realistic costs and the nearer-purchase glide path.

The client is a 25-year-old professional with $50,000, a five-year first-home horizon and a 4–5% annual net return objective. The low end of that target is the model hurdle. The benchmark is BIL, a short Treasury-bill ETF proxy. The Lab 14 proposed universe adds IEF (intermediate Treasuries), LQD (investment-grade credit), and VT (global equities). BIL must be at least 20%, weights are fully invested and long-only, and the proposed equity cap declines from 25% at five years to 0% at purchase. LQD plus VT is capped at 10% as a conservative corporate-issuer bound for unlevered holdings. Confirm actual exposures and derivatives policies before trading. At least 90% must be available within five business days, which requires evidence on execution, settlement, and withdrawal timing. The research cannot certify that gate.

The 90/10 mix clears the 4% hurdle by about 20 bp at the 20 bp incremental annual drag. At a 50 bp drag, the best modeled net estimate is 3.90%, with no qualifying mix. At one year to purchase it is 3.83%, also with no qualifying mix. With 36 monthly returns instead of 59, the minimum-risk choice becomes 100% BIL at 4.17% estimated net. These results show sensitivity to costs, horizon and the estimation window. The model's annualized volatility is a dispersion measure and does not bound loss at the purchase date.
