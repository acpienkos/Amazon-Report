# Amazon.com, Inc. (AMZN): Company Research Report

**Decision call: Watch-defer**  
**Primary filing:** Amazon.com, Inc. Form 10-K for the year ended December 31, 2025, filed February 6, 2026  
**Purpose:** Decide whether Amazon is worth continuing with for a full Project 1 valuation

## What the company does

Amazon makes money by selling products from its own inventory, collecting fees and fulfillment revenue from third-party sellers, and providing services including AWS cloud computing, advertising, subscriptions, digital content, and publishing. Management organizes the company into three segments: North America, International, and AWS. ([Amazon 2025 Form 10-K, Item 7, pp. 20 and 23](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1018724/000101872426000004/amzn-20251231.htm))

I view Amazon as more than an online retailer. Its retail businesses provide enormous scale and customer reach, while AWS, advertising, third-party seller services, and subscriptions add service revenue. AWS is especially important because it produces a large share of Amazon's operating income.

## What is going well

Amazon's total net sales increased 12% in 2025 to $716.924 billion. North America sales increased 10%, International sales increased 13%, and AWS sales increased 20%. Management attributed AWS growth mainly to increased customer usage, partly offset by pricing changes associated with long-term contracts. ([Amazon 2025 Form 10-K, Item 7, pp. 23–24](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1018724/000101872426000004/amzn-20251231.htm))

Profitability also improved. Consolidated operating income increased from $68.593 billion in 2024 to $79.975 billion in 2025. AWS produced $45.606 billion of 2025 operating income, compared with $29.619 billion from North America and $4.750 billion from International. ([Amazon 2025 Form 10-K, Item 7, p. 27](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1018724/000101872426000004/amzn-20251231.htm))

In my view, AWS is Amazon's strongest operating-value driver. Its sales growth accelerated from 13% in 2023 to 19% in 2024 and 20% in 2025. The earlier filings consistently explain that increased customer usage drove AWS growth, which suggests the recent improvement was connected to demand rather than only an acquisition or accounting change. ([Amazon 2023 Form 10-K, Item 7, p. 24](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1018724/000101872424000008/amzn-20231231.htm); [Amazon 2024 Form 10-K, Item 7, p. 24](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1018724/000101872425000004/amzn-20241231.htm); [Amazon 2025 Form 10-K, Item 7, p. 24](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1018724/000101872426000004/amzn-20251231.htm))

## What concerns me

Amazon's investment requirements increased much faster than sales. Cash capital expenditures rose from $48.1 billion in 2023 to $77.7 billion in 2024 and $128.3 billion in 2025. Management said these expenditures mainly supported technology infrastructure—most of it for AWS growth—and fulfillment capacity. It also expected both areas to increase again in 2026. ([Amazon 2023 Form 10-K, Item 7, p. 22](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1018724/000101872424000008/amzn-20231231.htm); [Amazon 2024 Form 10-K, Item 7, p. 22](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1018724/000101872425000004/amzn-20241231.htm); [Amazon 2025 Form 10-K, Item 7, p. 22](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1018724/000101872426000004/amzn-20251231.htm))

That spending affected current cash generation. Amazon's reported free cash flow was $36.813 billion in 2023, $38.219 billion in 2024, and $11.194 billion in 2025. Amazon defines this non-GAAP measure as operating cash flow minus cash purchases of property and equipment, net of proceeds and incentives, and warns that the measure has limitations. ([Amazon 2023 Form 10-K, Item 7, pp. 31–33](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1018724/000101872424000008/amzn-20231231.htm); [Amazon 2024 Form 10-K, Item 7, pp. 31–33](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1018724/000101872425000004/amzn-20241231.htm); [Amazon 2025 Form 10-K, Item 7, pp. 29–30](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1018724/000101872426000004/amzn-20251231.htm))

Management explicitly expects technology and infrastructure spending to increase over time and acknowledges that this can reduce short-term free cash flow. The company is adding infrastructure and employees for AWS and artificial-intelligence initiatives in pursuit of long-term growth. ([Amazon 2025 Form 10-K, Item 7, p. 20](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1018724/000101872426000004/amzn-20251231.htm))

My concern is not that Amazon is investing; AWS growth and operating income provide a reasonable business case for investment. The unanswered question is whether the future cash flows generated by these projects will justify the amount and pace of spending.

## Financial position

Amazon reported $86.810 billion of cash and cash equivalents at December 31, 2025. Cash, cash equivalents, and marketable securities together totaled $123.029 billion. ([Amazon 2025 Form 10-K, Item 8, Consolidated Balance Sheets and Note 3](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1018724/000101872426000004/amzn-20251231.htm))

Amazon also reported $68.836 billion in total face value of long-term debt, including a $2.748 billion current portion; the balance sheet classified $65.648 billion as long-term debt after unamortized discounts and issuance costs. ([Amazon 2025 Form 10-K, Item 8, Note 6—Debt](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1018724/000101872426000004/amzn-20251231.htm)) I used 10.827 billion diluted weighted-average shares because diluted shares include the potential effect of outstanding stock awards. ([Amazon 2025 Form 10-K, Item 8, Note 1—Earnings per Share](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1018724/000101872426000004/amzn-20251231.htm))

These figures will matter in the later valuation. The required enterprise-to-equity bridge will be: **equity value = enterprise value + cash − debt**. I have not yet estimated enterprise value or a value per share.

## Risks, challenges, and advantages

Amazon's advantages include its scale, broad selection of products and services, large fulfillment network, and AWS platform. Management focuses on lowering prices, improving availability and delivery speed, expanding selection, and investing in technology to strengthen the customer experience and operating efficiency. ([Amazon 2025 Form 10-K, Item 7, p. 20](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1018724/000101872426000004/amzn-20251231.htm))

Its challenges include intense competition, uncertain returns on large investments, dependence on continued demand for cloud and online services, changing tariffs and trade policies, inflation and interest rates, foreign-exchange movements, regulation and litigation, inventory management, and the execution risk involved in expanding fulfillment and data-center capacity. ([Amazon 2025 Form 10-K, Item 7, p. 20 and Item 1A, Risk Factors](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1018724/000101872426000004/amzn-20251231.htm)) In 2025, operating income also included $2.5 billion related to an FTC settlement and $2.7 billion of estimated severance costs, showing that regulatory and organizational issues can have measurable financial effects. ([Amazon 2025 Form 10-K, Item 7, p. 27](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1018724/000101872426000004/amzn-20251231.htm))

## Performance against its own history

Amazon's operating performance improved substantially over the last three filings. Consolidated operating income rose from $36.9 billion in 2023 to $68.6 billion in 2024 and $80.0 billion in 2025. At the same time, AWS growth accelerated and all three operating segments were profitable in 2025. ([Amazon 2024 Form 10-K, Item 7, p. 27](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1018724/000101872425000004/amzn-20241231.htm); [Amazon 2025 Form 10-K, Item 7, p. 27](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1018724/000101872426000004/amzn-20251231.htm))

The cash-flow trend is less clearly positive. Management said in the 2023 filing that it expected capital expenditures to increase in 2024, and the 2024 filing said they would rise again in 2025. Both predictions occurred: expenditures climbed from $48.1 billion to $77.7 billion and then $128.3 billion. The 2025 filing now says AWS and fulfillment investment should increase again in 2026. ([Amazon 2023 Form 10-K, Item 7, p. 22](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1018724/000101872424000008/amzn-20231231.htm); [Amazon 2024 Form 10-K, Item 7, p. 22](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1018724/000101872425000004/amzn-20241231.htm); [Amazon 2025 Form 10-K, Item 7, p. 22](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1018724/000101872426000004/amzn-20251231.htm)) This history makes management's current spending outlook credible, but it also makes the eventual return on those investments central to the valuation.

## Outlook and unresolved question

Management expects continuing investment in artificial intelligence, AWS infrastructure, and fulfillment capacity. It warns that technology spending can hurt short-term free cash flow, while arguing that the spending supports long-term growth. ([Amazon 2025 Form 10-K, Item 7, pp. 20, 22–23](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1018724/000101872426000004/amzn-20251231.htm))

**Partner's question:** What is the biggest risk that could hurt Amazon's future growth?

**My answer:** Amazon's biggest risk is that it continues spending heavily on AWS and AI infrastructure, but those investments do not generate enough future growth and cash flow to justify their cost.

I still need to determine a defensible long-term AWS growth rate, how much of Amazon's current investment will continue, an appropriate discount rate, and a terminal value. Those inputs will determine whether the strength of the operating business is already fully reflected in the valuation.

## Decision

**Watch-defer — wait for more information before making a decision.** Amazon is growing, especially through AWS, and its operating income has improved significantly. However, capital expenditures rose sharply and free cash flow fell in 2025. I need to build the DCF and test whether AWS growth and Amazon's other cash flows can justify the company's continuing investment requirements before making an initiate-buy decision.

**What would change my call:** I would move toward initiate-buy if a reasonable DCF using a slower long-term AWS growth rate shows that Amazon can fund its continuing investments and still generate enough free cash flow to support a value meaningfully above its market price.
